Intro

Way2Terrain began as an entry for the Dirt Jam. I debated using Unity or Godot, but went with Godot — it’s the recommended FOSS option, and the reference code was already written for it by Acerola, complete with helpful comments.

I had two concept ideas:
1) Way2Geeked — cloud rap vibes, experimental, geeked out.
2) Hueco Mundo but I’m already tackling that in another Unity project, so I shelved it.

Naturally, I went with Way2Geeked and built this moodboard to lock in the aesthetic. The spark came while I was listening to music and stumbled across the “Way2Geeked” visualizer on YouTube. All I could say 'bout it: “this shit trim, luh twin.”

Development

Way2Terrain came together by diving headfirst into topics I’d never touched before. Engines like Unity and Unreal have luxuries like built-in shader includes, auto shader recompilation, and polished terrain systems. Godot… doesn’t — which meant I had to learn exactly what was happening under the hood.


Technical Challenges

  • • GPU Data Alignment – I learned the hard way that forgetting to pad a struct to keep it 16-byte aligned can turn your shader uniforms into pure garbage. GPUs like data in vec4-sized chunks, so a vec3 needs a dummy float to fill the gap.

    • Shader Modularity – Built a GDScript-driven GLSL include loader, cutting ~500 lines of duplicated shader code.

    • Fog & Lighting – Implemented exponential fog using Beer's Law after a string of failed math experiments. Lighting got an upgrade too with a new Blinn-Phong model.

    • Texturing – After multiple failed OpenGL/Vulkan binding attempts, a tip from another jam dev unlocked proper sampler binding and UV mapping. End result: triplanar mapping, multiple texture size support (128p to 4K), and no more stretching issues.

    • Water Shader – Fragment-based displacement with Fresnel reflections, integrated into Sky3D.

    • Post-Processing – Lens dirt, film grain, and Filmic tone mapping to push the mood.

Conclusion

Way2Terrain ended up being both a technical playground and a creative statement — proof that a game engine can be just as much about expression as simulation. However so the journey wasn’t without heartbreak... most notably, I missed the official submission deadline due to an incomplete itch.io page and a broken build. Still, I walked away with far more than screenshots:

  • Start builds and deployment testing early.
  • Respect the time sink of low-level systems; scope accordingly.
  • Sleep is a feature, not a bug.
