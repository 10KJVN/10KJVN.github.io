Intro

This feature was originally planned as an overlay minimap with a Fog of War system, using a custom shader to manage three texture inputs: the base map, a fog texture, and a mask texture to reveal explored areas.

While we didn't manage to complete the system during our time on the project, this ended up being a valuable learning opportunity where i explored render textures, blend materials, and custom draw logic using the GPU. The work laid the technical foundation for a later team to implement the full system.

This setup was intended to dynamically reveal areas explored by the player.

Development

After brainstorming with my teammates, we considered several methods for rendering the minimap and Fog of War.

I wasn't yet sure how to do this, so i experimented with shader code and materials. Through trial and error, i discovered the limitations of Camera.Blit in that context and pivoted toward an alternative: drawing directly onto a texture using a brush and blend shader.

Each of us vaguely knew what had to be done, but which way would be the most effecient? I suggested setting up an extra camera that screenshots the whole map of each level through blitting. The only issue with this was, i had no idea how to do this specifically, so i went on ahead and experimented a little.

I wrote some throwaway shader code that was supposed to get the Main Camera in the scene as a component, then i litteraly wrote 'Camera.Blit' thinking that was a thing. It wasn't so at that point i hit a roadblock in my thinking process due to my limited knowledge at the time.

2 classes on the camera

After a lot of research and brainstorming, we asked our technical director for advice.
I explained to him i couldn't find out how to blit a whole level with the camera.

Conclusion

Although the full Fog of War system wasn't completed during our time on the project, our work laid the groundwork for its future implementation. We built a functioning prototype capable of drawing to a canvas using a RenderTexture and a custom brush system.

This system allowed the player’s position to be dynamically painted onto the map each frame. I also experimented with mapping world space coordinates to texture space, allowing for accurate player placement on the minimap across levels of different sizes. While my scaling logic was not pixel-perfect, it achieved a visually effective result.

This feature was eventually handed off to another developer, who likely rebuilt the system using a different approach. Or might've expanded on it, guess we'll never know. Still, this prototype was a great opportunity to expand my understanding of shader-based rendering and render pipeline behavior — and it provided a hands-on experience with integrating multiple systems: shaders, textures, cameras, and gameplay logic.
