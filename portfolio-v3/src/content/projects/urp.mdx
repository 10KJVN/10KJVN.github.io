## Custom Post-Processing

I implemented a Tint Renderer Feature with custom volume overrides.
This allowed designers to tint the entire screen with adjustable strength.
Two scripts work together to apply it: `TintRendererFeature` and `CustomPostScreenTint`.

<Split ratio="3:2" align="center">
    <div>

        ### Renderer feature

        A new `TintPass` is created and assigned, and the assigned pass is then enqueued to the renderer in use.
        This is the injection of the pass into the graphics pipeline.

    </div>
    <Media lightbox
           src="/images/urp/tint-renderer-feature-code.png"
           alt="TintRendererFeature and TintPass code"

</Split>

<Split ratio="3:2" reverse align="center">

        ### Volume override

        `CustomPostScreenTint` adds a float and a color to the inspector, with booleans to turn each on or off.

### What went wrong

I was stuck for two weeks getting the screen tint to render at all.
The shaders were fine, I had simply forgotten to add my renderer feature to the renderer.

## Chromatic Aberration

The effect started as an experiment in UV space, eventually evolving into a full post-processing pass with tweakable intensity.

<Gallery cols={2}>
    <Media lightbox
           src="/videos/urp/chromatic-aberration-object.gif"
           alt="ShaderGraph prototype splitting RGB channels on a single object"
           caption="ShaderGraph prototype: RGB channel offset per object." />
    <Media lightbox
           src="/videos/urp/chromatic-aberration-fullscreen.gif"
           alt="Fullscreen chromatic aberration effect"
           caption="Fullscreen effect: RGB channels split across the entire screen with customizable offset." />
</Gallery>

The UV-space version applies the effect within the bounds of a 3D object rather than screen-wide.
Each channel is manipulated based on altered UV input, simulating color fringing in a localized, object-contained way.

<Media lightbox
       src="/images/urp/shadergraph-uv-prototype.png"
       alt="ShaderGraph with hand-drawn annotations comparing the old and new UV offset setup"
       caption="Localized UV-space chromatic aberration prototype." />

Both implementations separately manipulate and recombine the color channels to form new RGB vectors,
then blend them with a sampled source using a strength-controlled `EffectMultiply` parameter.

<Media lightbox
       src="/images/urp/shadergraph-fullscreen-final.png"

       caption="Final fullscreen chromatic aberration graph. The annotations show how the RGB channels are offset and recombined." />

## Exploration

After delivering the core features, I kept exploring shader techniques:

- Color bleeding via blurred offsets
- Depth testing using normalized values
- Multipass rendering
- Color spaces, manually converting between RGB and YUV

Most of these resulted in glitchy, trippy visuals, but each experiment revealed something new about the rendering pipeline.

## Takeaway

This project was a turning point where I delivered two production-ready URP features.
It gave me hands-on experience with post-processing, custom renderer features, and shader architecture.
It built a foundation that continues to shape my path.

It showed me how shader math, rendering theory and artistic direction come together, and confirmed I wanted to keep exploring the technical art side of game development.
