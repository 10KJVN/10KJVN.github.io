## Overview

        *"The fair won't be here forever."*

        The Kermis is a game concept set at a Dutch fairground.
        It began with a fascination for temporary experiences: the idea that a moment's limited lifespan is part of what makes it meaningful.

        The original concept was to let players experience a world filled with small attractions before it eventually disappeared,
        leaving them with the memories they had made.

## Research / From Concept to Vertical Slice

<Split ratio="1:1">
    <div>

        I started by researching real fairground attractions, with a focus on shooting-stall variations:

        - Shooting nails, where the nails move
        - An RNG game: cutting for 2 euros
        - Shooting fingers: the whole finger has to come off, otherwise it doesn't count
        - A boxing-ball game for inspiration

        However, the initial ambition was broader, with multiple carnival attractions contributing to the experience.
        As development progressed, I reconsidered how much of that scope was necessary to demonstrate the game's core idea.

    </div>
    <div>

        After comparing them I chose the shooting gallery, then worked out the stall model and its in-engine setup, and wrote model specifications and art outsourcing requests.

        If the stall is large enough for three sections, I can make three variations, each with one open spot.
        In the first, left and right are occupied so you shoot in the middle, and so on.
        The prize is a bear, made into a prefab.

        However I chose to concentrate on one playable attraction instead.
        That gave me more room to iterate on the gameplay itself, test it with other people, and respond to what made the experience enjoyable.


    </div>
</Split>

## The Shooting Gallery

<Split ratio="2:3" align="center">
    <div>

        The shooting gallery became the project's main gameplay focus.
        It offered a concrete place to experiment with aiming, shooting, feedback, and the rhythm of a short minigame.

        I made the star targets breakable in Blender and tested them in game.
        The first version that sort of worked only aimed linearly, so you couldn't really aim at anything except the middle.

        My checklist for a playtest MVP (29 May):

        - Plastic-breaking sound effect
        - Rifle fire sound effect on every shot
        - Score counting
        - Balancing the stall prefab size and the shooting oscillator
        - A marker or UI element showing where you aim
        - Collisions for the stall
        - Maybe let the cans fall when shot

        ### What changed after testing?

        - **Scope:** A larger collection of attractions became a focused vertical slice.
        - **Priorities:** More attention went towards making the central gameplay enjoyable.
        - **Narrative:** The relationship between the playable experience and the original fascination remained an area to develop further.

    </div>
    <Media src="/images/caustics.svg" alt="Breakable star targets" caption="Breakable stars" />
</Split>

<Split ratio="1:1">
    <div>

        ### Medals

        I settled on three score tiers:

        - Bronze: 1000 points
        - Silver: 1500 points
        - Gold: 2500 points

    </div>
    <div>

        ### Difficulty

        Difficulty modifiers come from the targets' Rigidbody: mass, and potentially gravity.
        I managed 2000 points, and reached 2600 more easily with less mass.

        - Mass 1.0: hard
        - Mass 0.8: medium
        - Mass 0.75: easier

        Fairground games are unfair anyway, so I settled on 0.75.

    </div>
</Split>

## Systems

<Split ratio="1:1">
    <div>

        ### Interaction

        For an object to be interactable it needs a box collider, because that is what the player raycasts against.
        Without colliders on the model, the mouse-world raycast went straight through the building and tried to aim into the skybox, where my max reach of 300 made it fail.
        Placing proper colliders fixed it.

    </div>
    <div>

        ### Camera and game modes

        A main camera with a `CinemachineBrain` blends to any virtual camera it finds at runtime.
        Enabling the shoot camera switches to it, and disabling it switches back to the dominant camera.
        That was a temporary, manual solution, and automating it is next.
        When the timer reaches 0, a Unity Event exits shooting mode and re-enters explore mode.

        To stop the player walking off into the void if disabled mid-input, I plan to call `ClearState()` in `PlayerController.OnDisable`.

    </div>
</Split>

### HUD thinking

The ammo meter was a placement puzzle.
Under the aim marker doesn't work with this point of view, so I considered under the timer (since you're watching the time anyway) and bottom right next to the weapon, possibly as a vertical meter.
I wanted to ask someone who plays a lot of shooters, or a UI/UX artist, where they'd expect it.
The exploration HUD is intentionally minimal: coins, a time-of-day indication with hard-coded times (16:00, 18:00, 20:00), and maybe a pause button.
