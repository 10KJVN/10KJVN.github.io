## Overview

<Split ratio="1:1" align="center" reverse>
    <div>
        *"The fair won't be here forever."*

        The Kermis is a solo game project set on the final day of a Dutch fairground.
        What started as an idea for a collection of small attractions eventually became a playable vertical slice,
        centred around a shooting gallery.

        The project explores the experience of revisiting a familiar place,
        taking part in its attractions, and knowing that by tomorrow, it will all be gone.
    </div>

## Concept

<Split ratio="1:1" align="start">
    <div>
        The starting point for `The Kermis` was a fascination with atmosphere.
        I found that term too broad to build a game around,
        so I started exploring what actually interested me about it.
        Eventually, I realized it wasn't simply the atmosphere itself,
        but the memorable moments it can create, and the feeling of wanting to experience them again.
    </div>
    <div>
        During the concepting phase, I discussed a few different directions with my buddy group.
        One conversation in particular made me reconsider the setting:
        if temporality was such a big part of my fascination,
        wouldn't a kermis be a more fitting subject than a permanent amusement park?
    </div>
</Split>

<Center>
    I had explored a related fascination in an earlier project,
    `Weather Report`, albeit through a different approach.
    This time, I wanted to explore that interest in a more interactive way.
</Center>

## Vertical Slice

<Split ratio="1:1" align="start">
    <div>
        I started looking more closely at what made these fleeting experiences memorable.
        One distinction that helped me put it into words was the difference between
        `Vergankelijke Schoonheid` and `Efemere Pracht.`
    </div>
    <div>
        The former is more about the melancholy of things `gradually passing:`
        autumn leaves, aging, or something slowly falling apart.
        The latter puts the emphasis on the brief but intense `moment itself:`
        fireworks, a flower that only blooms for a day, or rain catching the sunlight.
    </div>
</Split>

<Center>
    Where `Weather Report` explored the former, I wanted `The Kermis` to lean towards the latter.
    I also visited the kermis in Burgermeester in 't Veldpark, Zaandam, to experience the attractions and observe the atmosphere firsthand.
</Center>

<Center width="md">
    Looking at the attractions up close,
    I started imagining what a day at the kermis might look like from the player's perspective.
</Center>

<Split ratio="1:1" align="start">
    <div>
        My initial idea was to let players spend a day at an amusement park with different companions,
        visiting attractions and taking pictures along the way.
        As the day passed, the surroundings and people would gradually disappear,
        leaving only the memories of that visit.
    </div>
    <div>
        I wasn't planning to simulate every attraction in full.
        Rides could instead be triggered through interactions and play out as short sequences,
        keeping the focus on the overall experience rather than the mechanics of each ride.
    </div>
</Split>

<Split ratio="1:1" align="start">
    <div>
        Further discussions with my peers pushed me to explore more interactive directions.
        Rather than only witnessing these moments,
        I wanted players to actively take part in them.
    </div>
    <div>
        This gradually evolved into a collection of smaller attractions and minigames,
        with players choosing how to spend their time.
        At one point, I even considered limiting them to three coins,
        forcing them to choose which attractions to visit.

        The idea was still broader than the final scope,
        but it gave me a more tangible direction to work towards.
    </div>
</Split>

## Shooting Gallery

<Split ratio="1:1" align="center">
    <div>
        I was considering three possible minigame directions:
        `RNG`-based, timing-based (like a boxing machine),
        or aiming-based.

        A quick vote among my peers put the shooting stall on top.
        It was also one of my own favourites,
        so I decided to build around it.

        I chose shooting stars as targets,
        giving the mechanic room for visual feedback and destruction.
    </div>
    <Media
    lightbox aspect="16:9"
    src="/images/kermis/InspoCollage.jpg"
    alt="Collage of kermis shooting attraction references"
    caption="References for presentation, targets and destructive feedback"
    />
</Split>

<Split ratio="1:1" align="center" reverse>
    <div>
        ### Prototyping

        I adapted a 2D hold-and-release projectile mechanic from a `Code Monkey` tutorial to a 3D scene,
        using it to test how the player could charge and fire their shots.

        Most of the iteration went into aiming.
        I tried firing along the camera's forward direction,
        using a fixed direction,
        and aiming towards the mouse's world position.

        `Pirate's Rampage` in `Final Fantasy VII Rebirth`
        helped shape the minigame's first-person framing and HUD,
        particularly its timer and score layout.
    </div>

<Split ratio="1:1" align="center">
    <div>
        ### Destructible targets

        I also prepared the star targets for destruction.
        Using Blender's `Breakable` modifier,
        I split the models into fragments and integrated them into the shooting mechanic.
    </div>
    <div>
        One unexpected behavior was that fragments could collide with neighboring stars and break them too.
        I kept this accidental chain reaction.
        Larger stars were worth more points,
        and could lead to more destructive reactions.
    </div>

## Playtesting

<Center width="md">
    The first playtest exposed a few issues with the shooting gallery.
    Players struggled to identify shootable targets,
    aiming didn't always feel accurate,
    and the trajectory line could obstruct the actual shot.
</Center>

<Split ratio="1:1" align="start">
    <div>
        ### Target readability

        The targets needed a clearer visual hierarchy.
        Larger stars received brighter emissive materials,
        and were worth more points to reward players for prioritising them.
    </div>
    <div>
        ### Aiming and hit detection

        I experimented with different collider configurations and camera angles.
        Eventually, I settled on sphere colliders to make hits more consistent.
        I also set the camera's `FOV` to `80°`.
    </div>
</Split>

<Center width="md">
    A second playtest with 13 testers brought up a few more improvements:
    a proper end screen, clearer feedback when the timer ran out,
    faster charging and a projectile trail.
</Center>

<Split ratio="1:1" align="start" reverse>
    <div>
        ### Reusable responses

        I didn't make the entire environment shootable.
        Instead, I developed a reusable hit-response component.

        Different objects could react to shots with optional score,
        sound and visual feedback,
        without needing a separate implementation for each one.
    </div>
    <div>
        ### Gameplay feedback

        I implemented the projectile trail and added the result screen.
        An impact marker was also suggested,
        but didn't make it into the final build.

        The feedback also encouraged me to explore more reactive objects.
        A stuffed animal, for example,
        could simply play a squeak when hit.
    </div>
</Split>

## Final Experience

<Center width="md">
    The shooting gallery became the main playable attraction,
    but I still wanted the introduction and ending to frame it as one last visit to a place that's about to disappear.
</Center>

        ### Narrative framing

        The opening establishes the player's return to a place they remember from childhood,
        but no longer quite recognize.

## Reflection

<Center width="md">
    The biggest challenge with `The Kermis` was translating an abstract fascination
    into an experience that actually communicated it.
</Center>

<Split ratio="1:1" align="start">
    <div>
        ### *What worked*

        I turned the original concept into a playable vertical slice.
        The playtests also helped me improve the shooting gallery's usability,
        from target readability to aiming and feedback.
    </div>
    <div>
        ### *What I missed*

        During the final review,
        one question stood out:
        was the project actually about `anti-nostalgia`,
        rather than the ephemeral splendor I had intended?

        The reviewer also felt I had moved from concept to production too quickly,
        without deeply analyzing the kermis experience itself.
    </div>
</Split>

<Center width="md">
    > I had tested whether the gameplay worked,
    > but not whether the intended emotional experience came across.
</Center>

<Split ratio="1:1" align="start">
    <div>
        ### *Looking back*

        In hindsight,
        I had spent more time developing the attraction than investigating what made the setting meaningful.
        Most of my playtesting focused on usability,
        rather than the thematic and emotional aspects of the experience.
    </div>
    <div>
        ### *Next time*

        I'd spend more time observing and analyzing the subject before committing to production.
        I'd also make room for testing the intended feeling of the experience,
        alongside its mechanics and usability.
    </div>
</Split>
